# Student-specific Latin phonetics: evidence and draft mapping

Prepared: 2026-11-03. Status: **core sound mapping established; conversion edge cases remain for implementation**.

Purpose: guide a future switch between the app's Russian phonetic spelling and Latin phonetic spelling familiar to this Spanish-speaking student. This is a record of spelling evidence, not an assessment of the student's spoken pronunciation or a generic Spanish transliteration standard.

## Confirmed user decisions (2026-11-03)

- Use **ch** consistently for the sound written **х** in the app, including that sound in ח and כ/ך. This is a user-selected normalization; the notes themselves use both j and ch.
- Use the German chart only to fill **צ/ץ → z** (chart: Z, “wie in zehn”) and **ז → s** (chart: S, “wie in See”). Keep existing mappings for all other sounds, including **в → v**, **й → y**, **ш → sh**, **х → ch**, and **с → s**. The resulting profile deliberately mixes conventions; it is not a full German transliteration system.
- **Keep stress marks**, transferring the app's acute accent to the corresponding Latin vowel. Example: `hоле́х` → `holéch`.

## Rules and limits for the later implementation

1. Map known pronunciation, not bare Hebrew letters. ב, כ, פ, ש, ו, י and ה can have different roles; silent final ה must not automatically produce h. This file does not supply a Hebrew vocalization algorithm.
2. Keep `h` distinct from the chosen spelling of х. The app's search equivalence between h and х is not a pronunciation rule.
3. **Known ambiguity accepted by the chosen scope:** both /z/ (ז; app з) and /s/ (ס/שׂ; app с) produce `s`. Do not silently change the existing с mapping to ss or import German context rules. The chart offers S/SS for /s/, but that part was not selected. Latin text alone cannot reliably recover this sound distinction. For illustration, app `зе` → `se`, while app `сэ́фер` → `séfer`; these are derived examples, not photo quotations.
4. Treat `sh` and the selected `ch` as units. Do not apply replacements recursively or interpret Latin j as the й sound.
5. Context-sensitive Cyrillic combinations (я, ю, ё, й, ь, ъ) need a separate, reviewed conversion policy. The photos do not justify blindly mapping every я to ya, every soft sign to an apostrophe, or every й to y. Use the actual Hebrew pronunciation and preserve vowel sequences.
6. **User confirmed: keep stress accents.** Transfer the app's stress information to the Latin vowel, e.g. `hоле́х` → `holéch`. P3 also contains acute accents in `Oméret` and `noldá`, although the notes are inconsistent. Use the app's reviewed pronunciation as the stress source; do not copy every handwritten accent or omission.
7. Do not reproduce apparent lesson-note errors as rules: ambiguous `Ejch`, apparent `Octobus`, omitted letters, or uncertain vowel spellings need review. `Shabbat` does not establish that doubled Hebrew consonants should always be doubled in Latin.
8. Spaces, hyphens, capitalization, and syllable breaks vary in the notes. A consistent display policy must be chosen separately; none is established here.
9. No app code, dictionary entries, UI date, or search behavior is changed by this document. This is a mapping specification, not an executable converter; contextual Cyrillic conversion and borrowed sounds still need separate handling.

## Remaining limits

- No more photos are required to establish the core consonant mappings: the user-selected chart fills צ/ץ and ז. Keep h, v, y, sh, and the other existing mappings; do not reopen them based on the German chart.
- Borrowed sounds צ׳ / ז׳ / ג׳ are absent from the chart and remain unestablished; resolve them only if needed by the future feature.
- Vowel sequences, Cyrillic я/ю/ё and ь/ъ, and consistent spacing need implementation rules grounded in the app's pronunciation data. The chart is not authority for changing these under the current request.
- Preserve the distinction between observed note spellings and user-selected output conventions. In particular, z for ц and s for з come from P5 and the user's instruction, not from the four handwritten pages.

## Approved implementation policy (2026-11-03)

The user approved these conversion rules for the Spanish localization:

- Use pronunciation-based Latin spellings and omit Russian soft/hard-sign markers: `ульпа́н → ulpán`, `бинья́н → binyán`, `носъи́м → nosím`, `тию́ль → tiyúl`.
- Preserve actual consonantal y and vowel sequences: `каньо́н → kanyón`, `меанье́н → meanyén`, `ба́аль → báal`. Do not interpret every soft sign as y.
- Preserve existing spaces, hyphens, form separators, and stress. Latin accented vowels may be stored in NFC form.
- Keep the original pronunciation's single/doubled consonants rather than deriving doubling from Hebrew spelling.
- Store reviewed Latin pronunciations in each entry's `translations.es.pronunciation`. Do not transliterate Russian explanations or generate phonetics at render time.
- Use the same Latin conventions in Spanish card notes. Explain the personalized conventions in the Spanish dictionary introduction.
- The current 224 entries contain no borrowed ч/ж sounds. Resolve their spelling with the user if later additions require them.

The evidence and historical uncertainties above remain useful provenance; this section resolves the implementation choices explicitly approved by the user.
